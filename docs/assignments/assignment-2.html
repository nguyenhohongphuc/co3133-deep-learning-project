Problem statement

Given one photo of a Vietnamese dish, predict which of 30 dishes it shows. Such a classifier is the first step of food-logging, menu-search and tourism apps. Input: one RGB photo of any size. Output: one of 30 labels. The prediction unit is the image. We train with cross-entropy on the logits f(x) ∈ ℝ30 and predict ŷ = argmax f(x).

Main difficulties:

  • Fine-grained classes. Several dishes share ingredients and differ only in shape or broth: Banh chung (square) vs. Banh tet (round); Bun bo Hue vs. Bun rieu vs. Banh canh.
  • Web-crawled photos. Sizes, framing and backgrounds vary widely, and duplicates and wrong labels occur.
  • Moderate imbalance (3.4× between the largest and smallest class).

Research question

How much does ImageNet pretraining help over a small CNN trained from scratch on about 17 k food photos? Within fine-tuning, does updating the whole backbone beat training only a new head, and is the gain worth its compute cost?

Dataset description and EDA

30VNFoods (Kaggle, version 11, MIT license; paper) has 25,136 JPEG photos of 30 dishes, crawled from the web, with one label per image given by its folder. The download is 4.3 GB. The source URL of each image is listed in Urls/, so copyright of each photo stays with its source. All 25,136 files decode and are RGB.

EDA notebook: notebooks/a2_eda.ipynb. It runs end-to-end on the local copy and writes every figure and table below.

Class distribution and imbalance

  • Train per class: from 310 (Banh pia) to 1,071 (Bun bo Hue), mean 586; imbalance 3.45×. The counts fall off gradually and no class is rare, so we report macro-F1 and per-class F1. We keep plain cross-entropy for now; weighted cross-entropy is a candidate ablation.
  • Smallest class after cleaning: 306 train / 44 validation / 89 test images.

Input size and aspect ratio

  • Median size 1000×720 px (min 100×83, max 7360×5184). 24.6% are exactly square.
  • 1.4% (251) have a short side below 224 px. They are spread over all classes (at most 2.8%, Canh chua), so we keep them.
  • 1.1% (193) have an extreme aspect ratio (> 2 or < 0.5). They are real dish photos, but a centre crop can cut off the dish. We keep them and will check whether they fail more often (hypothesis H3 below).

Colour and brightness

  • Train v2 mean RGB [0.582, 0.514, 0.400] and std [0.269, 0.265, 0.293]. The images are warmer and brighter than ImageNet (mean [0.485, 0.456, 0.406]).
  • Class medians lie between 0.41 and 0.57 and the boxes overlap, so brightness alone does not separate dishes. Banh pia is the brightest class, because many of its photos show packaging on white backgrounds. This is a possible shortcut (H4).

Duplicates and label noise

  • Exact copies (MD5): 63 groups covering 129 images. 40 groups span two splits; 38 carry different labels.
  • Near duplicates (pHash, 64-bit): 427 clusters of ≥ 2 images. 222 span two splits; 56 carry different labels. pHash catches 402 of the 507 removed images; MD5 alone would miss them.
  • The label conflicts concentrate on the pairs we expect to confuse: Bun bo Hue / Bun rieu, Banh chung / Banh tet, Banh duc / Banh gio, Banh can / Banh khot. One is a clear error: a goi cuon photo labelled Banh mi.

  • Leakage rule. Two images are linked when their pHash distance is ≤ 6, or ≤ 8 within one class. We picked the threshold by inspecting pairs at distances 2, 4, 6 and 8. At distance 8 the number of cross-label pairs jumps to 18, mostly false matches. The largest cluster has 3 images, so there is no chaining.
  • Cleaning rules: drop every cluster with two labels (118 images); for a cluster across splits, keep one copy in the priority order Test > Validate > Train (196 removed); for a cluster inside one split, keep one copy (193 removed). Each removed image, its reason and the copy kept are listed in results/a2/tables/a2_drop_list.csv.
  • Training transforms: RandomResizedCrop(224, scale=(0.08, 1)), horizontal flip, ColorJitter(0.1, 0.1, 0.1). Evaluation: Resize(256) + CenterCrop(224). JPEGs are decoded at reduced size (PIL draft, ≥ 320 px) to speed up loading.
  • Normalisation: the CNN uses Train v2 statistics; ResNet-50 uses ImageNet statistics, which its pretrained weights expect.
  • Tensor shapes: images [B, 3, 224, 224] float32, labels [B] int64 in 0–29.

Methodology

Pipeline overview

30VNFoods JPEGs + split_v2.json -> decode (PIL draft) -> augment / resize-crop -> normalise
  -> DataLoader (batch 64 CNN / 32 ResNet, 6 workers) -> model -> 30 logits -> cross-entropy
  -> Adam -> argmax -> accuracy, macro-F1 on validation -> keep best checkpoint

The images (4.3 GB, median 1000×720 px) do not fit in memory, unlike Fashion-MNIST in A1. They are read from disk by six worker processes. Each worker decodes a JPEG straight to about 320 px using PIL draft mode before cropping, which keeps the GPU fed. The training loop is the A1 trainer (src/engine/trainer.py), reused unchanged. All A2 code is in src/a2/, so the A1 results stay reproducible.

Why the A1 CNN had to change: at 224 px, the A1 design (2 blocks + flatten) would put about 6 M parameters into the linear head (56×56×64×30), and each output would see only about a 10 px patch. Four blocks and global average pooling give a larger receptive field and a 7.7 k-parameter head.

Fine-tuning procedure

  • The optimizer has two parameter groups (src/a2/trainer.py): the new 30-way head always uses lr 1e-3, and the backbone uses lr_backbone. The treatment is frozen versus full backbone adaptation, including the associated gradient and BatchNorm modes.
  • Frozen (resnet50_frozen, lr_backbone = 0): every backbone parameter has requires_grad = False, and the backbone stays in eval mode so BatchNorm keeps its ImageNet statistics. Only the head (61,470 parameters) is trained.
  • Full (resnet50_full, lr_backbone = 1e-4): all layers are trained; the backbone learns 10× more slowly than the head so the pretrained features are adapted, not overwritten.
  • Both arms: 224 px input, the same augmentation, Adam with weight decay 1e-4, batch 32 (the full arm does not fit batch 64 in 8 GB at fp32, so the frozen arm uses 32 as well), at most 15 epochs, early stopping on validation macro-F1 with patience 5. Weights are downloaded by torchvision on first use.

Full fine-tuning wins on every seed, by 0.122 macro-F1 on average. That gap is about 40 times the seed-to-seed spread of either arm (≤ 0.003), supporting a consistent gap across the observed seeds. Epochs are counted from 1. Peak memory is torch.cuda.max_memory_allocated (tensors only; nvidia-smi shows more because of the CUDA context and allocator cache).

  • Low variation over the observed seeds. Within-arm variation is much smaller than the frozen/full gap; this observation is limited to three seeds.
  • Frozen: learning slows within the tested budget. Training and validation loss stay close (0.78 vs. 0.82 at epoch 15), with small late macro-F1 gains. This does not prove a hard capacity limit or rule out improvements from another optimization setup.
  • Full: fast, then starts to overfit. Macro-F1 is 0.82 after one epoch, already above the frozen arm's best, and levels off near 0.88 from epoch 4. Validation loss is lowest between epochs 4 and 10 and then creeps up while training loss keeps falling (0.25–0.30 vs. about 0.50 at the end). Early stopping ended two of the three runs. A learning-rate schedule or stronger regularisation is the obvious next step, but it would be a separate experiment.

Training behaviour of the CNN baseline

  • CNN: underfitting, not yet converged. Training loss is still falling at epoch 20 (1.77), and the best checkpoint is at epoch 18, so 20 epochs are not enough. Training loss stays above validation loss because training images are heavily augmented (random crops down to 8% of the image) and pass through dropout. Validation scores swing by up to 0.09 between epochs (0.556 at epoch 18, 0.462 at epoch 20). A likely cause is the constant learning rate of 1e-3 with no schedule; we will add one in M3.

Current test inference was measured on Apple MPS with PyTorch 2.8.0, batch 32 and no loader workers: CNN 0.859 ms/image, frozen 4.312 ms/image and full 4.285 ms/image (means over the available runs). These are amortized forward-only times from one streamed pass after one warmup batch, excluding data loading, transfers and CSV export. They are not repeated batch-1 latency benchmarks. Frozen and full have the same inference architecture; these small timing differences do not establish a speed advantage. The RTX training costs above and Apple inference costs are measurements on different devices.

Comparison and discussion

On the validation set:

  • The pretrained ResNet baseline performs better. Even with a frozen backbone, ResNet-50 beats the CNN by about 0.21 macro-F1 (0.763 vs. 0.556), while training only 61 k parameters, about one sixth of the CNN's 397 k. Architecture and capacity also differ, so this comparison does not isolate pretraining. The CNN number is still one draft run, so this gap is indicative.
  • Full fine-tuning adds 0.122 macro-F1 over the frozen backbone (0.885 ± 0.002 vs. 0.763 ± 0.003, 3 seeds each, frozen versus full adaptation). These results support adaptation under this protocol without isolating why ImageNet features differ from the target domain. The cost is 2.1× the training time per run and 6.7× the GPU memory, which is affordable on an 8 GB laptop GPU, so full fine-tuning is the model we carry forward.
  • Final test: mean macro-F1 is 0.5403 (CNN), 0.7720 (frozen) and 0.8922 (full). CNN versus ResNet changes architecture/capacity as well as initialization, so this comparison does not isolate pretraining alone. How far the CNN would get with a different training budget remains untested.

Error analysis

The following diagnostic counts refer to the validation-selected full run, seed 44: 525 errors in 4,984 images. They are distinct from the three-seed aggregate above. Error groups may overlap. Image observations and proposed causes were recorded for 23 inspected examples; suspected label ambiguity does not automatically change labels.

Ablation study (mandatory for A2)

The controlled experiment above is our mandatory ablation. Removing backbone adaptation (backbone lr 1e-4 → 0, everything else fixed) lowers validation macro-F1 from 0.885 ± 0.002 to 0.763 ± 0.003 over 3 seeds (results section, Figure 5).

Limitations and conclusion

  • Limited baseline and seed coverage: both ResNet arms have three-seed final test results; CNN remains a single draft run. Subgroup/error illustrations use one validation-selected run per family.
  • No learning-rate schedule: the full arm starts to overfit after about 4–10 epochs; a schedule could raise its score and was not tested here.
  • Source leakage not checked: photos from the same website may still sit in different splits. Urls/ might map images to domains, but we have not verified that mapping.
  • Label noise remains beyond the conflicts revealed by duplicates.

For these fixed checkpoints, full backbone adaptation improves mean test macro-F1 from 0.7720 to 0.8922 at about twice the training time per run; inference architectures and costs remain similar. Fine-grained soup confusions and high-confidence mistakes remain. H3 is not supported by this test subgroup, while H4 remains unresolved. This conclusion applies to the cleaned split and current protocol; the original uncleaned test set is not reported.
